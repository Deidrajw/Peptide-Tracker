# Nexus — Timing, Escalation and Dose-Related Content Audit
**Date:** 2026-10-23 | **Status:** REVIEW DRAFT — NOT CLINICALLY APPROVED / NOT IMPLEMENTED

## Scope / source of truth
Audited UI strings rendered by `peptideCardHTML()` in `assets/js/08-nexus.js` from design branch `design/v0.9.6-protocol-card-density`, chiefly Retatrutide-specific timing, phase, escalation and injection-site information. Source's `assets/js/01-nexus.js` config sets Retatrutide `halfLifeDays:6` and `troughDay:5.5`; no individual pharmacokinetic measurements are obtained. This audit does **not** modify clinical claims in the current UI; it records suggested changes for a separate approved medical-content release.

## Evidence
- Lilly FAQ (medically reviewed Sep 2026): retatrutide is investigational, used only in clinical trials; not approved by any regulatory agency; its efficacy and safety are being studied. https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide
- Jastreboff et al., NEJM (2023) Phase 2: estimated half-life approximately six days, supports once-weekly administration. Trial escalation regimens were administered per study protocol at four-week intervals. This does not justify the app deciding an individual is ready to increase dose from weeks alone. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2301972
- Apple App Review Guidelines, §§1.4.1 and 1.4.2 (as accessed Oct 2026): medical apps may receive greater scrutiny and drug dosage calculators must originate from specified approved institutions or have regulatory approval. A generic disclaimer, renaming or removing the word *medical* does not automatically meet this policy. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- FDA, Device Software Functions Policy Navigator Step 7: preserving user-entered schedule/history and non-prescriptive prompting is distinct from offering patient-specific treatment or dosing suggestions. Classification is function-specific, not guaranteed by a product category label. https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/step-7-does-device-software-functions-dsf-and-mobile-medical-applications-mma-guidance-apply
- Health Canada, Software as Medical Device guidance: lifestyle tracking and patient records may fall outside medical-device regulation, but treatment/management function must be assessed separately. https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/software-medical-device-guidance-document.html

## Acceptance criteria before any public App Store submission
1. Complete string inventory beyond this file (`assets/js/01-nexus.js` guides and `Intel`, dose preparation/reconstitution outputs, Stash, warnings and calculators), with source and clinical reviewer decisions.
2. Distinguish *recorded* dose intervals and user-entered facts from unmeasured drug levels, predictions and treatment readiness. Never imply the model measured plasma concentrations.
3. For investigational drugs, do not encourage self-directed dose escalation. Explicitly state investigational status accurately in educational contexts.
4. Require review of Apple §1.4.2 drug dose calculator provenance and applicable regulator classification before submission, even with separate general wellness branding. Preserve calculators pending explicit product decision and compliance review.
5. Test all amended text and any alert priority in LIGHT/DARK 320px/390px, screen-reader, locale and long text. Preserve symptom/intensity, log/edit, source/vial maths and localStorage.
6. Do not merge proposed replacement text automatically as part of the visual bottom-sheet release. First obtain content approval and clinical/regulatory review.

**Current status:** Findings and sample replacements are research-informed proposals, not diagnosis, dosage guidance, regulatory clearance, legal advice or a comprehensive clinical assessment.
